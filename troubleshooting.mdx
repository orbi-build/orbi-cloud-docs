Start at the status page: the **Next** card's headline says what state you are in, and anything waiting on you is listed under **Needs your attention** with the button that fixes it.

Almost every failure state in Orbi Cloud is self-describing. This page covers what each one means and what to do, in roughly the order people hit them.

## I do not know what to do next

Look at **Setup progress** at the top of the status page. The highlighted step is the one waiting on you, and its **How to do this step ▶** link plays a short video of exactly that step. The [quickstart](/quickstart) walks the same seven steps in writing.

If your repository is connected and its work environment is ready but nothing has started yet, the **Next** card lists open Issues from your repository. Click **Let Orbi do this** on one of them, or click **Write a request** and describe the change in a sentence. See [your first Issue](/first-issue#dispatching-from-the-status-page) and [write a request](/write-a-requirement).

## Nothing happens after I label an Issue

Work through this list, in order:

1. **Does the Issue actually carry `ai-ready`?** This is the most common cause. Writing the Issue is not enough; the label is what starts the work. Check the Issue's labels on GitHub. The status page's **Let Orbi do this** button and the write-a-request page's **Hand off to Orbi** add the label for you.
2. **Is it in a connected repository?** Orbi only reads Issues in the repositories you connected. Check the project overview on the status page.
3. **Is the work environment ready?** **Prepare work environment** in **Setup progress** must show **Done**. Until then there is nothing to run the delivery.
4. **Has five minutes passed?** Adding the label usually wakes Orbi within a minute; the five-minute timer is the fallback.
5. **Did the label come back off, with a comment?** Your free deliveries are used up — see [limits and quotas](/limits-and-quotas#when-the-allowance-runs-out). Subscribe, then re-apply the label.
6. **Does the page say Monthly AI usage is used up; new tasks are paused?** New Issues wait until the allowance resets, or until you add your own model key. The labels stay. See [limits and quotas](/limits-and-quotas#when-the-monthly-quota-runs-out).
7. **Does the page say A task is not in the current version and will not start yet?** A current version (delivery scope) is set, and your Issue is not in that version's milestone. Move it into the version, or switch the current version. See [releases](/releases#the-delivery-scope).
8. **Does the Issue already carry another `ai-*` label?** An Issue that is already `ai-in-progress` or `ai-merged` is not re-dispatched by adding `ai-ready` again.

If the **In progress** tab says work environment preparation failed, the reason is under **Usage and settings** → **Connected projects**.

## `ai-blocked`

`ai-blocked` is a deliberate stop, not a crash. It means automatic recovery was not safe, and a person has to decide the next step. The status page says **Orbi is stuck and needs you** and names the Issue.

**Read the comment Orbi left on the Issue.** It states why it could not proceed. When Orbi recorded a reason, the card on the status page sums it up in one sentence and its **See Orbi's note** button opens that comment; otherwise the button is **View on GitHub (Issue #N)**. The common causes:

- The repair loop ran its rounds without reaching a clean review verdict.
- An external precondition is missing — something the delivery cannot safely judge or fix on its own.
- A release's milestone does not exist. The status page offers **Create the milestone and continue** for this one.
- The Issue asks for something the environment cannot do (see below).

**How to recover:** fix the underlying cause first, then re-apply `ai-ready`. Fixing the cause often means editing the Issue — sharpening the acceptance criteria, removing an impossible requirement, or splitting it into smaller Issues.

<Note>
Removing the `ai-blocked` label on its own does not restart anything, and neither does commenting. Repair the cause, then re-apply `ai-ready` — that is what puts the Issue back in the queue.
</Note>

The pull request, branch and workspace are all preserved when an Issue blocks, so nothing you have is lost.

### Acceptance criteria the environment cannot satisfy

A frequent cause of a blocked Issue is asking for evidence that cannot exist inside the delivery environment. That environment has your repository and your tests — it does not have your production credentials, your third-party accounts, a real browser against your live site, or your deployment pipeline.

An Issue whose acceptance requires "deploy to production and confirm", "log in to the live dashboard and screenshot it", or "reproduce against the real expired token" will spin and then block. Rewrite the criterion as something checkable in the repository — assert the rendered text in a test, assert the error classification, assert the thing that must not appear — and gather the production evidence yourself.

## Orbi needs more information

The task shows **Needs detail** (`ai-needs-detail`), and the status page shows a card saying the Issue needs more detail. Click **See Orbi's question** to read what is missing, then **Add the details**. That opens the write-a-request page; what you settle there is added to the Issue body, and the Issue goes back to `ai-ready` on its own.

You can also do it on GitHub: answer the question by editing the Issue body, then re-apply `ai-ready`. Orbi re-reads the Issue body when it picks the Issue up again, so a reply in a comment alone is not enough.

## The pull request is waiting for my approval

The headline says **You need to approve this change on GitHub**, and the task shows **Awaiting approval**. Your branch protection requires an approving review, which Orbi cannot give itself. Approve the pull request, or change the rule — see [branch protection](/connect-repository#branch-protection-that-blocks-every-merge).

## The pull request looks wrong

Comment on the Issue with the correction, or edit the Issue body. A running delivery picks it up and restarts with your correction — you do not need to close anything or cancel. See [steering a delivery](/delivery-lifecycle#steering-a-delivery-that-is-already-running).

If the work is simply not wanted, close the Issue. If it was a free delivery, closing it without a merge refunds the slot.

## Provisioning failed

The **Next** card says whether it needs you, is retrying, or is being recovered by the platform, and **View failure reasons and repair steps** takes you to **Usage and settings** → **Connected projects**, which shows the reason, the time, an **Error ID** and the next step. There are four classes, covered in full on the [provisioning](/provisioning#when-provisioning-fails) page:

| Reason | What to do |
|---|---|
| The GitHub App installation or authorization is no longer valid | **Reauthorize the GitHub App**, then come back to the status page |
| The API key was rejected | Correct your key with **Update model configuration**; saving retries automatically |
| Unable to connect to the AI model service (the API key or baseUrl may be wrong) | Check the base URL and key, use **Test connection**; saving retries automatically |
| Work environment preparation failed (on Orbi's side) | Nothing. It recovers automatically; check back later |

Quote the **Error ID** if you contact support. The support email link next to it puts the Error ID in the subject for you.

## I cannot connect my repository

| What you see | Fix |
|---|---|
| The repository is not in the list | Open **Cannot find your repository?**, add the repository under Repository access in **GitHub App settings**, click Save, then come back and refresh |
| **(Issues disabled; enable them first)** after the repository name, or **Cannot connect repository** because Issues are disabled | Enable Issues under the repository's **Settings → Features → Issues**, then click **Back and retry** |
| **Repository limit reached** | Disconnect a repository you no longer need first (**Disconnect a repository**); on Free and Solo you can also upgrade to Pro |
| The repository list could not be loaded from GitHub | Refresh later; if it keeps failing, type the repository into the field below as `owner/repository` |
| Invalid repository format | Enter it as `owner/repository` |

Full detail is on [connect a repository](/connect-repository#what-the-errors-mean).

## The GitHub App was uninstalled

The status page detects this and says **Orbi's GitHub App was uninstalled. Reinstall it to continue.**, offering **Reinstall the Orbi GitHub App**. Reinstalling on the same account reconnects to your existing tenant — your history and settings are intact. You may need to reconnect the repository afterwards.

## Issues got disabled on my repository

If the Issues feature is turned off after connecting, the status page says so. Re-enable **Settings → Features → Issues** — Orbi's entire input channel is Issues, so nothing can run without it.

## My quota is draining faster than expected

Open **Usage and settings** and check the **Cached** percentage on the **Monthly AI usage** line. Cached tokens count toward the allowance the same as input and output tokens, so the cached share does not change how fast the allowance is used; a fast-draining allowance means the tasks themselves use a lot of tokens. The usage line under each task in **Task history** shows which deliveries were expensive.

Large, vague Issues cost far more than small, specific ones — both in tokens and in repair rounds.

## A task shows no usage

If a task in **Task history** has no usage line, that work item never reported usage statistics. It is a gap in measurement, not a sign the delivery failed. The **Monthly AI usage** line notes how many such tasks are left out of the total.

## My session expired

Sign in again. Nothing is lost: your repository connections, model configuration and task history belong to your tenant, not to the browser session.

## The page is in the wrong language

Open the account menu (your avatar, top right) and choose **🇨🇳 ZH** or **🇺🇸 EN**. On the sign-in page, the same switch is in the top right. The choice is stored for a year on that browser.

## Getting help

| For | Where |
|---|---|
| Questions, something not working, how-to | The Telegram group — **Join the Telegram group for support** in the account menu, or **Telegram group** in the footer of every Orbi Cloud page. The public channel is [t.me/orbi_build](https://t.me/orbi_build) |
| Billing, refunds, account and privacy | Email **support@orbi.build**, or use **Email us** in the account menu |
| A bug you are happy to report in public: **Report a bug (GitHub, public)** in the account menu or the footer. A reproducible bug in the open-source engine goes here too | [GitHub Issues on orbi-build/orbi](https://github.com/orbi-build/orbi/issues) |

There is no guaranteed response time, but a report with the right details is answered much faster. Include:

- What you did and what you expected.
- The Issue or pull request URL.
- The **Error ID** if a provisioning failure is involved.
- What the status page says: the exact wording of the headline and of the item under **Needs your attention**.

Never paste an API key, a token, or any credential into a support message, an Issue or a pull request.

If a page here disagrees with what the product shows, that is worth reporting too — open an issue on [orbi-build/orbi-cloud-docs](https://github.com/orbi-build/orbi-cloud-docs/issues).
