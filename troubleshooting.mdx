Start at the status page: the **Next** card's headline says what state you are in, and anything waiting on you is listed under **① Needs you** with the button that fixes it.

Almost every failure state in Orbi Cloud is self-describing. This page covers what each one means and what to do, in roughly the order people hit them.

## I do not know what to do next

Look at **Setup progress** at the top of the status page. The highlighted step is the one waiting on you, and its **How to do this step ▶** link plays a short video of exactly that step. The [quickstart](/quickstart) walks the same seven steps in writing.

If you are connected and provisioned but have not started anything yet, the quickest move is **Let Orbi make a starter Issue → Try →** in the **Next** card — see [your first Issue](/first-issue#dispatching-from-the-status-page).

## Nothing happens after I label an Issue

Work through this list, in order:

1. **Does the Issue actually carry `ai-ready`?** This is the most common cause. Writing the Issue is not enough; the label is what starts the work. Check the Issue's labels on GitHub.
2. **Is it in a connected repository?** Orbi only reads Issues in the repositories you connected. Check the repository overview on the status page.
3. **Has provisioning finished?** **Provision environment** must be **✓ Done**. Until then there is nothing to run the delivery.
4. **Has five minutes passed?** Pickup is on a timer, not instant.
5. **Did the label come back off, with a comment?** Your free deliveries are used up — see [limits and quotas](/limits-and-quotas#when-the-allowance-runs-out). Subscribe, then re-apply the label.
6. **Does the page say Monthly quota exhausted?** New Issues wait until the allowance resets, or until you add your own model key — the labels stay. See [limits and quotas](/limits-and-quotas#when-the-monthly-quota-runs-out).
7. **Does the page say Orbi is standing by, with an Issue outside the current delivery scope?** A delivery scope is set, and your Issue is not in that version's milestone. Move it into the version, or switch the scope — see [releases](/releases#the-delivery-scope).
8. **Does the Issue already carry another `ai-*` label?** An Issue that is already `ai-in-progress` or `ai-merged` is not re-dispatched by adding `ai-ready` again.

If the **In progress** tab is empty and says the environment is not ready, the answer is under **Settings and reference → Connected repository**.

## `ai-blocked`

`ai-blocked` is a deliberate stop, not a crash. It means automatic recovery was not safe, and a person has to decide the next step. The status page says **Orbi is stuck and needs you** and names the Issue.

**Read the comment Orbi left on the Issue.** It states why it could not proceed; the status page summarises the reason and links to it. The common causes:

- The repair loop ran its rounds without reaching a clean review verdict.
- An external precondition is missing — something the delivery cannot safely judge or fix on its own.
- A release's milestone does not exist. The status page offers **Create the milestone and continue** for this one.
- The Issue asks for something the environment cannot do (see below).

**How to recover:** fix the underlying cause first, then re-apply `ai-ready`. Fixing the cause often means editing the Issue — sharpening the acceptance criteria, removing an impossible requirement, or splitting it into smaller Issues.

<Note>
Removing the `ai-blocked` label on its own does not restart anything, and neither does commenting. Repair the cause, then re-apply `ai-ready` — that is what puts the Issue back in the queue.
</Note>

The pull request, branch and workspace are all preserved when an Issue blocks, so nothing you have is lost.

### Acceptance criteria the environment cannot satisfy

A frequent cause of a blocked Issue is asking for evidence that cannot exist inside the delivery environment. That environment has your repository and your tests — it does not have your production credentials, your third-party accounts, a real browser against your live site, or your deployment pipeline.

An Issue whose acceptance requires "deploy to production and confirm", "log in to the live dashboard and screenshot it", or "reproduce against the real expired token" will spin and then block. Rewrite the criterion as something checkable in the repository — assert the rendered text in a test, assert the error classification, assert the thing that must not appear — and gather the production evidence yourself.

## Orbi needs more information

The Issue shows **Needs detail** (`ai-needs-detail`). Orbi's comment on the Issue says what is missing. Answer in a comment or edit the Issue body; you do not need to relabel.

## The pull request is waiting for my approval

The headline says **PR #N needs your approval**, and the Issue shows **Awaiting approval**. Your branch protection requires an approving review, which Orbi cannot give itself. Approve the pull request, or change the rule — see [branch protection](/connect-repository#branch-protection-that-blocks-every-merge).

## The pull request looks wrong

Comment on the Issue with the correction, or edit the Issue body. A running delivery picks it up and restarts with your correction — you do not need to close anything or cancel. See [steering a delivery](/delivery-lifecycle#steering-a-delivery-that-is-already-running).

If the work is simply not wanted, close the Issue. If it was a free delivery, closing it without a merge refunds the slot.

## Provisioning failed

The **Next** card says whether it needs you, is retrying, or is being recovered by the platform, and **View failure reasons and repair steps** shows the reason, the time, a **Request ID** and the next step. There are four classes, covered in full on the [provisioning](/provisioning#when-provisioning-fails) page:

| Reason | What to do |
|---|---|
| GitHub App installation or authorization is no longer valid | Reinstall or re-authorize the App, then retry |
| The model credential was not accepted | Correct your key in **Model config**; saving retries automatically |
| The model service could not be reached | Check the base URL and key, use **Test connection**; saving retries automatically |
| Platform-side failure | Nothing — it recovers automatically. Check back later |

Quote the **Request ID** if you contact support.

## I cannot connect my repository

| What you see | Fix |
|---|---|
| The repository is not in the list | Open **Cannot find your repository?** → **Manage App authorization**, add the repository to the installation, then reload |
| **(Issues are turned off — Orbi can't work here)**, or **Cannot connect repository** because Issues are disabled | Enable Issues under the repository's **Settings → Features → Issues**, then retry |
| **Repository limit reached** | Deactivate a repository you no longer need first; on Free and Solo you can also upgrade to Pro |
| The repository list was temporarily unavailable | Retry; use **Enter manually** if it persists |
| Invalid repository format | Enter it as `owner/repository` |

Full detail is on [connect a repository](/connect-repository#what-the-errors-mean).

## The GitHub App was uninstalled

The status page detects this and says **The GitHub App was uninstalled**, offering **Reinstall the Orbi GitHub App**. Reinstalling on the same account reconnects to your existing tenant — your history and settings are intact. You may need to reconnect the repository afterwards.

## Issues got disabled on my repository

If the Issues feature is turned off after connecting, the status page says so. Re-enable **Settings → Features → Issues** — Orbi's entire input channel is Issues, so nothing can run without it.

## My quota is draining faster than expected

Open **Settings and reference** and check the **Cache hit rate** next to **Monthly usage**. Cache-miss tokens cost about **50 times** more than cache hits, so a low rate burns the allowance dramatically faster. The per-work-item usage in **Delivery history** shows which deliveries were expensive.

Large, vague Issues cost far more than small, specific ones — both in tokens and in repair rounds.

## Usage shows `—`

That work item never reported usage statistics. It is a gap in measurement, not a sign the delivery failed.

## My session expired

Sign in again. Nothing is lost: your repository connections, model configuration and delivery history belong to your tenant, not to the browser session.

## The page is in the wrong language

Open the account menu (your avatar, top right) and choose **🇨🇳 ZH** or **🇺🇸 EN**. On the sign-in page, the same switch is in the top right. The choice is stored for a year on that browser.

## Getting help

| For | Where |
|---|---|
| Questions, something not working, how-to | The Telegram group — **Join the Telegram group for support** in the account menu, or **Telegram group** in the footer of every Orbi Cloud page. The public channel is [t.me/orbi_build](https://t.me/orbi_build) |
| Billing, refunds, account and privacy | Email **support@orbi.build** |
| A bug report or question you are happy to ask in public — **Open a GitHub issue** in the account menu or the footer, or a reproducible bug in the open-source engine | [GitHub Issues on orbi-build/orbi](https://github.com/orbi-build/orbi/issues) |

There is no guaranteed response time, but a report with the right details is answered much faster. Include:

- What you did and what you expected.
- The Issue or pull request URL.
- The **Request ID** if a provisioning failure is involved.
- What the status page says — the exact wording of the headline and of the item under **① Needs you**.

Never paste an API key, a token, or any credential into a support message, an Issue or a pull request.

If a page here disagrees with what the product shows, that is worth reporting too — open an issue on [orbi-build/orbi-cloud-docs](https://github.com/orbi-build/orbi-cloud-docs/issues).
