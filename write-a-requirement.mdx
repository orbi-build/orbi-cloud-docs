You don't have to write the Issue yourself. On the status page (the page you land on after signing in), click **Write a request** and say what you want changed in a sentence or two. Orbi reads the code in your project and writes a draft, usually within one to two minutes. Nothing is created in your GitHub repository until you review the draft and hand it off.

## Write and hand off

<Frame caption="The write-a-request page: describe the change in a sentence, or pick an example">
  <img src="/images/draft-entry.png" alt="The write-a-request page with a text box, the Draft the request button and three examples" />
</Frame>

1. On the status page, click **Write a request**. The button appears once a GitHub repository is [connected](/connect-repository). If you have connected several, use **Switch project** on the page to pick one.
2. Under **What would you like to change?**, describe it in plain words: a new feature, a bug fix, a text change. One sentence is enough; a longer description is fine too. Then click **Draft the request**.
3. Orbi reads your code and writes the draft. This usually takes one to two minutes, and the page shows **Writing the draft** meanwhile. For a project you have only just connected, Orbi first waits for its work environment to be [prepared](/provisioning), another minute or two. You can close the page; to come back, click **Write a request** on the status page and then **Open draft**. Sometimes Orbi asks you a few questions, either along with the draft or before writing it; see the next section.
4. Read the draft. It states what you asked for, what your users should see when it is done, what is left out, and how Orbi read anything your words left open.
5. Click **Hand off to Orbi**. This creates an Issue in your GitHub repository, and Orbi works from it. The page then shows **Handed off to Orbi** and **Usually starts within a minute**.

Two cases where the work does not start right away: you are out of free runs (see [Usage](#usage)), or the page shows **Not scheduled yet**. In the second case, follow the steps the status page shows, or [contact us](/troubleshooting#getting-help); nothing counts toward your free runs or quota while it waits, and you don't need to hand it off again.

## When Orbi isn't sure

If your request can be read more than one way, Orbi asks you rather than guessing. The questions and their options are listed under **Orbi chose these for you**, and Orbi has already picked the option it recommends for each. You will see one of two layouts:

<Frame caption="A draft with Orbi's question under it, the recommended answer already picked">
  <img src="/images/draft-question.png" alt="A draft followed by Orbi chose these for you and the Hand off to Orbi button" />
</Frame>

- **A draft with questions under it.** The draft already uses the recommended answers, so if you agree you can hand it off without changing anything. If an answer isn't what you meant, pick another option, or choose **Other (write your own)** and type your own. Then click **Rewrite the draft**; it takes about a minute, and you can hand it off once the new draft is ready. Changed your mind? Click **undo your changes**.
- **Questions with no draft yet.** Pick your answers the same way, then click **Write the draft**.

To change anything else, write it in the box under the draft and click **Send**. Orbi rewrites the draft, again in about a minute, and keeps what you said before, so you don't have to repeat it.

## What gets created

When you hand off, Orbi creates a normal GitHub Issue in your connected repository. Its first line says it was submitted by you through Orbi; that line is currently always in Chinese (由 @your-login 通过 Orbi 提交). After it comes the draft exactly as you approved it. On the status page it appears as a task, showing the Issue title.

Each project has one draft at a time. A draft you haven't handed off is kept for 7 days after you last changed it. Writing a new request in the same project replaces it, and the page tells you so before you start. For tasks you have handed off, the Issue stays in your repository either way.

## Usage

- Writing drafts doesn't use your free runs, and there is no separate limit on how many drafts you write.
- A task you hand off is counted the same way as an Issue you give Orbi any other way: on the free plan, a run only counts if the change is merged.
- **Free plan:** your free runs are a one-time allowance and do not reset monthly. When you have none left you can still write and hand off, but the task won't start. After you [subscribe](/billing), the task shows up on the status page among the Issues Orbi hasn't started; click the button next to it (such as **Try →**) to start it. You don't write it again.
- **Paid plans:** when this month's AI usage limit is reached, writing drafts stops too until the limit resets on the 1st of the next month. You won't be charged extra. See [Limits and quotas](/limits-and-quotas).

## When to write the Issue yourself

If you already know exactly what you want and can list how to check it, [creating the Issue on GitHub](/first-issue) is quicker. Write a request helps most when you know what you want solved but not where to change the code.
